# US Weekly Kickstart: The impact of the AI capex boom on S&P 500 return on equity

- **Following a volatile week, the S&P 500 has returned 9% YTD but trades at the same 21x P/E multiple as it did a year ago.** The S&P 500 YTD return reflects a 17% rise in consensus forward 12-month earnings estimates alongside a 7% decline in the P/E multiple, from 22x to 21x. The current 21x P/E ranks in the 87th percentile since 1980. Going forward, we expect earnings growth will continue to drive the S&P 500. Within the market, recent Momentum factor volatility has tracked with the historical precedent following sharp rallies.
- **Record profitability has been one of the factors supporting high S&P 500 valuations.** S&P 500 trailing 4-quarter ROE registers a record high 22%. Whether S&P 500 valuations revert toward their long-term average or prove structurally higher going forward will depend in part on the trajectory of corporate profitability. In our macro model, every 1 percentage point change in S&P 500 ROE is associated with about a 1x turn in S&P 500 P/E. Differences in profitability also help explain the variation in valuations across equity indices.
- **S&P 500 ROE has surged by 150 bp during the past four quarters, driven by a boost from the mega-cap technology stocks to index profit margins.** Among DuPont components, rising S&P 500 profit margins have also been the primary driver of the index's 800 bp ROE expansion since 1985. In recent quarters, ROE for the aggregate S&P 500 has benefited from the growing weight of the mega-cap tech stocks while ROE for the median S&P 500 company has declined. The seven largest tech stocks currently have a collective ROE of 44%, which has expanded by 900 bp during the past three years.
- **The AI capex boom has lifted semiconductor profitability but will be an increasing headwind to mega-cap tech ROE going forward.** The hyperscalers have grown in asset intensity and experienced declining sales/asset turnover since the start of the AI boom. During the next few years, rising hyperscaler depreciation expenses will reverse some of the S&P 500 ROE benefit that AI capex has generated in the form of elevated semiconductor profit margins. Equity raises to fund investment spending will also pressure ROE. Consensus estimates imply that ROE for the seven largest technology stocks will decline by an average of 700 bp next year.
- **AI also creates a key long-term upside case for ROE, both for the mega-cap tech stocks and for the broad equity market.** For the hyperscalers, the net impact of the AI capex boom on profitability will depend on the magnitude of returns on their investments. For the rest of the market, AI-related productivity gains should eventually lift ROE via higher asset turnover and potentially higher profit margins. However, the share of AI-related economic gains that accrue to companies will depend on a number of factors, including government tax policy. Falling effective tax rates have helped lift S&P 500 ROE during the past few decades, but the corporate profit share of US GDP is now at the highest level since 1950.
- **We rebalance our ROE Growth basket ([GSTHGROE](https://marquee.gs.com/s/products/MAHW93S13XB6VSBD/summary)) in this report.** The basket includes the 50 stocks with the highest consensus ROE growth during the next 12 months.

# The outlook for S&P 500 ROE

**Volatile market rotations during the past week continued to follow the pattern of the sharpest Momentum rallies in recent decades.** Before the sell-off that started last week, a narrow-breadth Momentum rally had driven the sharpest two-month S&P 500 return since 1971, when scaled relative to volatility. Following similarly sharp rallies in the past, the Momentum factor has usually struggled during the subsequent few months. In addition to the historical precedent, the current market characteristics of elevated trading leverage, still-narrow market breadth ([Exhibit 36](#_e493526e-ca97-4000-ab04-0fa4844ce000)), and uncertainty about both the macro outlook and the AI build-out suggest volatility [will persist](https://publishing.gs.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/06/10/de03eea4-cf6c-4a8a-8208-dd124801e134.html).

**Record profitability has been one of the factors supporting high S&P 500 valuations.** The current S&P 500 trailing 4-quarter return on equity ("ROE") is 22%, the highest on record. Although the P/E is elevated relative to history, it is roughly in line with the fair value indicated by our macro models. Just as investors typically assign higher valuation multiples to stocks with higher profitability, history suggests that the profitability of the aggregate equity market helps determine its P/E multiple, along with macro factors like interest rates and inflation.

**In contrast with surging S&P 500 Index ROE, ROE for the median S&P 500 stock has declined during the past few years and remains below previous highs.** Since 1985, ROE for the median S&P 500 company has expanded from 14% to 17%. However, during the past few years, ROE for the median stock has declined due to a combination of higher interest expense, lower leverage, and modestly higher tax expense.

**ROE for the aggregate S&P 500 has benefited from the growing weight of mega-cap tech stocks, which generate above-average profitability.** ROE for the seven largest stocks in the S&P 500 (NVDA, AAPL, GOOGL, MSFT, AMZN, AVGO, and META) currently registers a collective 44% and has expanded by 9 pp during the past three years ([Exhibit 13](#_b3c6f410-dfec-4000-a8e4-e72306164000)).

**The demand for chips has pushed semiconductor profitability to extremely high levels, but investors remain skeptical about the durability of those earnings.** The largest direct earnings beneficiaries of the AI capex boom have been semiconductor firms. Strong moats and a shortfall of supply relative to demand have given these semiconductor firms strong pricing power, boosting their margins and lifting ROE. However, investors fear the combination of slowing demand and increasing supply, including from new competitors, will eventually weigh on semiconductor profitability. Analyst forecasts imply that semiconductor companies will largely be able to defend profit margins during the next year, but their estimates imply a decline in ROE as growth in equity outpaces growth in earnings.

**The hyperscalers have also become increasingly asset-heavy businesses.** For the decade prior to AI capex build-out, the asset-light business models of the hyperscalers were reflected in rising sales/asset ratios, supporting ROE expansion. However, the physical footprint required to support AI workloads has led to a steady decline in asset turnover, which analysts expect will continue in 2027.

**Capital raising to fund the AI build-out signals additional headwinds to ROE.** Consensus estimates [suggest](https://publishing.gs.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/06/10/e4561f0d-7cdf-4116-8d38-08b96a5fd299.html) the hyperscalers will spend $770 billion on capex in 2026, equivalent to 100% of cash flows from operations. In order to fund continued capex growth, the companies have increasingly turned to debt and equity issuance and pulled back on buybacks. Net debt for the hyperscalers has increased by $170 billion since start of 2025 and share counts have begun to rise. While further increases in corporate leverage would support ROE expansion going forward, all else equal, higher leverage would also likely result in increased interest expenses, weighing on ROE.

**Despite growing headwinds, the net impact of the AI capex boom on the hyperscalers' ROE will depend on the magnitude of returns on their investments.** The Q1 earnings season [offered evidence](https://publishing.gs.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/05/14/9bf1965c-f804-4786-af35-cb530580ee10.html) of the hyperscalers' ability to monetize AI, with positive revenue revisions, large backlogs, and rising gross margins. Our equity analysts [also expect](https://publishing.gs.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/05/06/0b0ac4fa-18bb-468e-b19e-77c653f71cc4.html) an inflection in token economics ahead for hyperscalers and model providers, enabled by stabilizing token prices but falling compute cost per token.

**For most of corporate America, AI productivity represents a potential tailwind to profitability.** We remain in the early stages of enterprise AI adoption. Last quarter, roughly 54% of companies [discussed AI](https://publishing.gs.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/06/10/e4561f0d-7cdf-4116-8d38-08b96a5fd299.html) in the context of productivity on their earnings calls, but just 11% of companies quantified the AI productivity gains on a specific use case and only 2% of companies quantified the impact of AI productivity on earnings. The majority of companies focused on cost savings and efficiencies related to AI productivity. For the average company in the index, eventual AI productivity gains should boost sales and earnings per employee, in turn lifting profitability.

Lower corporate tax rates have been one of the largest contributors to ROE expansion during the past 40 years.
